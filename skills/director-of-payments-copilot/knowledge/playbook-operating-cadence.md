# Director of Payments Playbook Notes

## Thesis

Payments leaders win by making payment performance visible, explainable, and
actionable before failures compound.

The playbook is not just a metric list. It is an operating cadence:

- daily operations
- weekly business reviews
- monthly business reviews
- quarterly business reviews
- annual planning
- three-year strategy

## Core Narrative

When payments work, they are usually invisible. When payments stop, the whole
organization looks at the payments leader.

The job is to:

- catch and correct payment failures before they compound
- speak the language of the business, not only the language of payments
- connect payment outcomes to company goals
- build credibility across product, finance, support, fraud, and executives

## Daily Operations

Start with input and output KPI metrics.

Input metrics are levers the team can manage:

- authorization rates
- retry success rate by attempt number
- customer fix-up rate
- recovery rate across retries and customer outreach

Output metrics are business results:

- net sales volume
- payment success rate
- recovered orders
- cost of payments
- transaction volume
- customer contacts

Read all metrics by:

- payment method
- acquirer or processor
- region
- raw decline reason

Do not rely only on acquirer-translated response codes. Raw decline reason data is
closer to what the bank or network actually returned.

AI is positioned as a capacity multiplier for anomaly detection, trend
identification, and alerts.

## Weekly Business Reviews

The WBR is not a news report. It is a meeting to catch issues and drive action.

Recommended audience:

- subscription team
- checkout team
- merchandising team
- fraud team
- support or customer-service stakeholders
- anyone whose work feeds into the payment step

Best timing: Monday, reviewing the prior week and creating an action-oriented
start to the week.

Meeting structure:

1. What changed this week?
2. What is in our control and what is not?
3. What are we doing about it?

Signs the WBR is failing:

- the deck looks the same every week
- the group only shows metrics and moves on
- people are disengaged
- nobody asks to join

## Monthly Business Reviews

The MBR is a state-of-the-union update for mid-senior leadership.

The tone shifts from tactical coordination to strategic communication.

Frame around:

- what changed month over month
- what the payments team is actively working on
- what problems appeared
- how the team is addressing them
- how payment work maintains or reduces cost

Keep an 80/20 balance: mostly informing, with some guidance-seeking.

## Quarterly Business Reviews

The goal is to move payments from a slide in someone else's QBR to a dedicated
strategic session.

Progression:

1. Include payment data in existing QBRs.
2. Spend time with individual leaders explaining why it matters.
3. Build toward a dedicated payments session.

Success signal: leaders ask what they can do to accelerate payment work.

## Annual Planning

Annual planning defines what the next 12 months should look like for the payments
organization.

Start in late Q2 or early Q3 rather than waiting for Q4 budget season.

Plan structure:

- celebrate what was accomplished
- include industry changes, regulations, network updates, and consumer behavior
- group initiatives into themes
- tie each theme to output value: reduce cost, increase revenue, improve CX
- identify ambitious plays that should become funded initiatives

## Three-Year Strategy

Write the three-year strategy without current constraints:

- no budget constraints
- no technology barriers
- no headcount ceiling

Important tenets:

- every merchant should have multi-processor redundancy
- payment data ownership should remain with the merchant or platform
- AI will become a primary interface for payment operations teams

The test of a good tenet: every payment team member can articulate it, defend it,
and apply it to a company goal.
